Module 1

Trader psychology and the right mindset

Most accounts are not lost to bad charts. They are lost to a good plan abandoned in a bad moment. Start here.

1.1You trade probabilities, not predictions✓

Nobody knows what Bitcoin will do in the next hour. Not you, not the loudest account on social media, not an algorithm. What a trader can know is how a setup behaves over many trades.

Think of one trade as one card from a deck. A single card tells you nothing about the deck. A hundred cards do.

A series, not a single trade

A setup wins 45 times out of 100. Every win makes 2 units, every loss costs 1 unit.

  • 45 wins × 2 = +90
  • 55 losses × 1 = −55
  • Result after 100 trades: +35 units, while being wrong more often than right.

Inside that series you will still meet five or six losses in a row. That is normal arithmetic, not a broken strategy. If one losing trade can upset you, the position is too large.

Remember

Judge yourself on the next 50 trades, never on the last one.

1.2Fear, greed, FOMO and revenge✓

Four emotions do most of the damage, and each one has a recognisable shape:

  • FOMO. BTC is up 8% today and you buy the top of a green candle because "it's leaving without me". There is no stop, because there was no plan.
  • Fear. Your setup appears, but the last two trades lost, so you skip it. It wins. You then force the next, worse one.
  • Greed. The target is hit, but you cancel the take-profit to "let it run". Price comes all the way back.
  • Revenge. After a loss you double the size to win it back quickly. This single habit ends more accounts than any bear market.

You cannot switch emotions off. You can make rules that work while you feel them:

  • No plan written down, no trade.
  • Two losing trades in one day and the day is over.
  • Size never grows after a loss.
  • Wait for the candle to close before acting on it.
1.3Process over outcome: keep a journal✓

A good trade is one where you followed your plan. It can still lose money. A bad trade is one where you broke your rules. It can still make money, and that is the dangerous kind, because it teaches you the wrong lesson.

Write down every trade. Five lines are enough:

  • Why I entered (setup, timeframe).
  • Entry, stop, target and position size.
  • Result in R (see module 2).
  • Did I follow the plan? Yes or no.
  • What I felt when I entered and when I closed.

After 30 trades, read it back. Patterns show up that you cannot see trade by trade: "I lose most on Sunday nights", "my breakout trades work, my reversal trades don't".

1.4Realistic expectations✓

"Double your account every month" is a sales line, not a result. Some sober numbers:

Monthly return$1,000 after 1 yearAfter 3 years
3%$1,426$2,898
5%$1,796$5,792
100% ("guru" promise)$4,096,000not a real thing

A steady 3–5% a month would put you among very good traders, and even that will not arrive as a smooth line. There will be losing months.

For the first year, the goal is not profit. The goal is to still have your account and a journal full of trades you can learn from. Trade the smallest size your exchange allows. Tuition paid in $5 losses teaches the same lessons as tuition paid in $500 losses.

Remember

Survive first. Size up only after your journal, not your mood, says the method works.

Module 2

Risk management

The one skill that separates traders who last from traders who were right once. Every number here is simple arithmetic, so do it before each trade.

2.1The 1–2% rule✓

Risk a fixed small part of your account on each trade: 1%, and never more than 2%. "Risk" means what you lose if the stop-loss is hit. It is not the size of the position.

Why so small? Because losing streaks are certain, and the deeper the hole, the harder the climb out:

Risk per tradeAfter 10 losses in a rowGain needed to recover
1%−9.6%+10.6%
2%−18.3%+22.4%
5%−40.1%+67.0%
10%−65.1%+186.8%

Ten losses in a row at 1% is a bad month. At 10% it is the end of the account. The strategy is the same in both rows. Only the size is different.

Remember

You cannot control whether the next trade wins. You fully control how much it can cost.

2.2Position sizing: how much to buy✓

The stop decides the size, not the other way round.

Position size = money at risk ÷ stop distance

BTC long, $2,000 account
  • Risk 1% of $2,000 = $20
  • Entry $64,000, stop $62,720, so the distance is $1,280 (2%)
  • Size = $20 ÷ $1,280 = 0.0156 BTC, worth about $1,000

If the stop is hit you lose $20, exactly as planned. With a tighter stop at $63,360 (1% away) the same $20 risk buys 0.0313 BTC, worth $2,000.

Notice what happened: a wider stop means a smaller position and a tighter stop means a larger one. The dollar risk stays the same.

A note on leverage: it does not change this formula. Leverage only decides how much margin the exchange locks up. Trouble starts when people use leverage to take a size the formula never allowed.

2.3Stop-loss: the price where your idea is wrong✓

A stop is not a pain limit ("I can stand losing $50"). It marks the price at which the reason for the trade no longer exists.

  • For a long, that is usually just below the last swing low or below the support you are buying.
  • For a short, just above the last swing high or the resistance.
  • Leave a little room. Stops placed exactly on a round number or exactly on the low get hit by ordinary noise.

Then set the size from that distance, as in the previous lesson.

Three rules that keep stops honest:

  • Place it in the exchange when you enter. A "mental stop" disappears the moment it is needed.
  • Never move it further away. Moving it closer or to break-even is fine.
  • If a sensible stop makes the trade too big or too far, skip the trade. Not every chart offers one.
Careful

"It will come back" is not a plan. On the days it does not come back, an account without a stop is gone.

2.4Reward-to-risk and expectancy✓

Measure trades in R, where 1R is the amount you risked. Risk $20 and make $40: that is +2R. Stopped out: −1R.

The reward-to-risk ratio tells you how often you need to be right just to break even:

Reward : riskWin rate needed to break even
1 : 150%
1.5 : 140%
2 : 133.3%
3 : 125%

Expectancy is what an average trade earns:

(win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss)

Example

40% wins at +2R, 60% losses at −1R: 0.40 × 2 − 0.60 × 1 = +0.2R per trade. Over 100 trades at 1% risk that is about +20R, roughly +20% on the account, with 60 losing trades along the way.

So a high win rate is not the goal. A positive expectancy is. This is how the theory looks on this site's tracked AI signals right now:

2.5Loss limits and total exposure✓

Sizing one trade well is not enough if you hold five of them.

  • Correlation. Most altcoins fall when BTC falls. Three longs on BTC, ETH and SOL at 1% each are not three ideas. They are one idea at 3% risk.
  • Open risk cap. Keep the total of all open stops under about 3–4% of the account.
  • Daily limit. Down 3% in a day: close the platform. Nothing good is decided after that.
  • Weekly limit. Down 6% in a week: stop until Monday and reread the journal.
Example

$5,000 account, 1% risk = $50 per trade. Daily limit 3% = $150, which is three full losses. The fourth trade of that day does not happen, however good it looks.

Limits feel unnecessary on good days. They exist for the one day a month when you are tilted and do not know it.

Module 3

Reading charts and finding the trend

A chart is a record of who was willing to pay what. You do not need twenty indicators. You need to see direction, levels and whether the move has strength.

3.1Candles and timeframes✓

Each candle shows four prices for its period: open, high, low, close. The body runs from open to close. The wicks show how far price went before it was pushed back.

  • A long lower wick: sellers pushed down, buyers took it back.
  • A large body closing near its high: buyers were in control to the end.
  • Small bodies with wicks both ways: nobody is in control.

The same market looks different on each timeframe, so read from the top down:

  • 1D for the main direction.
  • 4H for the setup and the key levels.
  • 1H for the exact entry.
Example

On 1H, BTC looks like it is collapsing: five red candles from $66,000 to $64,500. On 1D that whole move is one small candle inside an uptrend. The 1H seller is trading against the bigger picture.

Wait for the candle to close. A candle that looks like a breakout with ten minutes left often closes as a wick.

3.2Market structure: which way is it going?✓

Trend is defined by swing highs and swing lows, nothing more complicated.

  • Uptrend: higher highs and higher lows.
  • Downtrend: lower highs and lower lows.
  • Range: highs and lows roughly level. No trend to follow.
Reading BTC structure

Low $58,000 → high $63,000 → low $60,500 → high $66,000.

Each high and each low is above the previous one, so this is an uptrend. It stays an uptrend while $60,500 holds. A close below $60,500 is the first lower low, and the long idea is no longer valid.

That last higher low is the most useful price on the chart. It tells you where to stand aside and it is the natural place for a stop.

The easy money is made trading with the structure of the higher timeframe. Buying pullbacks in an uptrend needs far less precision than calling the exact top.

3.3Support and resistance✓

Support is an area where buyers have stepped in before. Resistance is where sellers have. Four things worth knowing:

  • They are zones, not lines. Draw $62,800–$63,200, not $63,000.00.
  • Old resistance becomes support. After price breaks above $63,000 and returns to it, earlier sellers are gone and breakout buyers defend it. This "retest" is one of the cleanest entries there is.
  • Higher timeframe levels are stronger. A daily level outweighs a 15-minute one.
  • Levels wear out. Each touch uses up orders waiting there. The fourth or fifth test of a support breaks more often than the first.

Round numbers ($60,000, $70,000) and previous all-time highs act as levels simply because so many people watch them.

Remember

Mark three or four levels from the daily and 4H chart. If your chart has fifteen lines, it has none.

3.4Moving averages, volume and momentum✓

Indicators do not predict. They summarise what price already did, which is still useful as a filter.

  • 200-period moving average. Price above a rising 200 MA: look for longs only. Below a falling one: stay out or look for shorts. When the 200 MA is flat and price crosses it back and forth, the market is ranging and trend strategies lose money.
  • 20 and 50 EMA. In a healthy trend, pullbacks often stop near them. They give you an area to wait in, not a signal by themselves.
  • Volume. A breakout on volume well above average has participation behind it. A breakout on thin volume fails more often.
  • RSI. Above 70 does not mean "sell". In strong trends RSI stays high for weeks. It is more useful as a warning when price makes a new high and RSI makes a lower one.

The Hypr engine behind the AI signals on this site works on the same idea: it combines several of these factors and stays out when the market is not trending. Standing aside is a position too.

Careful

Adding a fifth indicator will not fix a missing stop or an oversized position. Module 2 matters more than this lesson.

Module 4

Swing trading: entries and exits

Holding trades for days to weeks. Slow enough to do next to a job, fast enough to learn from.

4.1What swing trading is, and when to stay out✓

A swing trader works on the 4H and 1D charts, holds for a few days to a few weeks, and takes perhaps two to six trades a month.

Why it suits most people better than day trading:

  • You check charts once or twice a day, not every minute.
  • Fees and spread are small next to a 5–10% move. For a scalper chasing 0.3% they are a large part of the profit.
  • There is time to think, so fewer emotional mistakes.

Knowing when not to trade is half the method:

  • Chop. Price moving sideways through a flat 200 MA. Trend setups fail here.
  • Big scheduled news. US rate decisions and inflation reports move BTC several percent in minutes. Either be in already with a stop or wait until after.
  • No clean level for a stop. Then there is no trade.
  • After your daily or weekly loss limit.
Remember

Cash is a position. A month with two good trades beats a month with twenty forced ones.

4.2Two entry setups that are enough✓

You do not need ten setups. Two cover most trending markets.

1. Pullback in a trend. The market makes a new high, then drifts back to a level: old resistance, the 50 EMA, or both. You buy when a candle closes showing buyers are back.

Pullback long

BTC is in a 4H uptrend, last high $66,000. It pulls back to $63,000, which was resistance two weeks ago. A 4H candle closes at $63,400 with a long lower wick.

  • Entry $63,400
  • Stop $61,900, below the pullback low (risk $1,500)
  • Target $66,400, just past the previous high (reward $3,000)
  • Reward : risk = 2 : 1

2. Breakout and retest. Price closes above a range, then comes back to test the top of the range from above. You enter on the retest, stop back inside the range.

Entering on the retest instead of the breakout candle means a closer stop, so a larger position for the same risk. You will miss the breakouts that never retest. That is an acceptable price.

Both setups mirror for shorts in a downtrend.

4.3Plan the trade before you enter✓

Every number is decided while you are calm, before any money is at risk. Seven lines:

  • Trend: which way is the daily?
  • Level: what am I trading from?
  • Trigger: what exactly must happen for me to enter?
  • Stop: where is the idea wrong?
  • Target: where is the next obstacle?
  • Size: from the formula in module 2.
  • Reward : risk: at least 1.5 : 1, or skip it.
The pullback trade, sized

Account $5,000, risk 1% = $50. Stop distance $1,500.

Size = $50 ÷ $1,500 = 0.0333 BTC, about $2,113 at the $63,400 entry.

  • Stopped out: −$50 (−1R)
  • Target hit: +$100 (+2R)

If the trigger never comes, there is no trade. If price runs away without you, there is no trade. Chasing it turns a planned 2 : 1 into an unplanned 0.7 : 1.

4.4Exits: taking profit and moving the stop✓

Entries get the attention. Exits decide the result. Decide them in advance too.

A simple, robust exit plan:

  • At +1R, close half and move the stop on the rest to your entry price. The trade can no longer lose.
  • Let the rest run to the target, or trail the stop under each new higher low.
  • Time stop. If nothing has happened after the period you expected, close it. Capital stuck in a dead trade has a cost.
What partials do to the result
  • Half closed at +1R, the rest reaches +2R: 0.5 × 1 + 0.5 × 2 = +1.5R
  • Half closed at +1R, the rest stopped at entry: +0.5R
  • Stopped before +1R: −1R

You give up some of the best case to make the average case calmer. Most people follow a plan better when part of the profit is already banked.

Module 5

Long-term investing and DCA

Trading is a skill you may or may not develop. Investing patiently in a sound asset asks for much less, and for many people it is the better plan.

5.1Investor or trader: keep two separate buckets✓

They are different jobs with different rules:

TradingInvesting
HorizonDays to weeksFour years or more
ExitStop-loss on every tradeNo stop, a thesis and time
LeverageSmall, if anyNone
AttentionDailyMonthly

Keep them in separate accounts. The classic mistake is a losing trade that gets renamed "a long-term hold" because closing it would hurt. That is not investing. That is a trade without a stop.

Before either bucket gets a dollar:

  • Three to six months of living costs sit in ordinary savings.
  • No high-interest debt. Paying off a 25% loan is a guaranteed 25% return.
  • Only money you will not need for at least four years goes into the investing bucket.

For the investing bucket, keep most of it in the largest, longest-tested assets (BTC, then ETH). Small speculative coins, if any, get a small share that you could lose entirely without changing your life.

5.2Dollar-cost averaging (DCA)✓

DCA means buying a fixed dollar amount on a fixed schedule, whatever the price. The same $100 buys more coins when the price is low and fewer when it is high, which pulls your average cost down.

$100 a month for five months
MonthBTC priceBTC bought
1$60,0000.001667
2$50,0000.002000
3$40,0000.002500
4$50,0000.002000
5$60,0000.001667

Total: $500 buys 0.009833 BTC. Average cost $50,847 per BTC, although the average price over those months was $52,000.

At the end BTC is back at $60,000 and the position is worth $590 (+18%). Someone who put all $500 in at month 1 is exactly at break-even.

Honest caveat: if the market only goes up from day one, a single purchase would have done better. DCA does not maximise returns. It removes the need to guess the timing, and it turns a crash from a disaster into a discount.

It only works if you keep buying when it is uncomfortable. Month 3 in the example is the purchase that made the result, and it is the one most people skip.

5.3Cycles and drawdowns: know what you signed up for✓

Bitcoin's long-term chart goes up. The path there has been brutal:

Peak → bottomFall from the peak
2013 → 2015about −85%
2017 → 2018about −84%
2021 → 2022about −77%

Every one of those took over a year, and at every bottom the news said it was finished. The past does not promise the future. It does tell you what kind of asset this is.

The test to do before you invest

You put in $10,000. Eighteen months later the app shows $3,000. Do you buy more, do nothing, or sell?

If the honest answer is "sell", your amount is too large. Reduce it until you could watch that number and carry on with your life.

What helps in a drawdown:

  • No leverage, so nothing can force you to sell.
  • A DCA plan that keeps buying at lower prices.
  • Money you do not need for years.
  • Not checking the price every day.
5.4Custody, security and taking profit✓

Keeping it safe. Coins left on an exchange are a promise from that exchange. Several large exchanges have failed and taken customer funds with them.

  • For long-term amounts, learn to use your own wallet. A hardware wallet is the standard choice.
  • Write the seed phrase on paper, keep it offline, in two places. Never photograph it and never type it into a website.
  • Nobody legitimate will ever ask for your seed phrase. Not support, not an "admin", not an airdrop.
  • Use an authenticator app for two-factor login, not SMS.
  • Send a small test amount before any large transfer.

Taking profit. "Never sell" is a slogan. Decide your rules while calm, and write them down:

  • Sell a fixed slice at set prices. For example 10% of the position each time price doubles from your average cost.
  • Or rebalance: if crypto was meant to be 20% of your savings and has grown to 40%, sell back down to 20%.
  • Decide what the money is for. A goal with a number is easier to act on than "more".
Remember

A plan made in advance beats a decision made at an all-time high, or at the bottom.

Module 6

Common mistakes

Almost everyone makes these, usually in this order. Reading about them is cheaper than paying for them.

6.1Too much leverage✓

Leverage multiplies the size of the position, and it brings the liquidation price closer in the same proportion:

LeverageMove against you that wipes out the margin
3xabout 33%
10xabout 10%
20xabout 5%
50xabout 2%

BTC moves 3–5% on an ordinary day. So at 20x an ordinary day is enough to take the whole margin, even if your direction turns out right an hour later. Exchanges close the position a little earlier than these numbers because of fees and maintenance margin.

Same idea, two outcomes

Long BTC at $64,000 expecting $68,000. Price first dips to $61,000 (−4.7%), then goes to $68,000.

  • Sized by the 1% rule with a stop at $60,500: the trade survives the dip and reaches the target.
  • $500 at 25x with no stop: liquidated near $61,500. Right idea, zero left.

If you use leverage at all, let the position sizing formula choose the size and let the stop, not the liquidation price, end the trade.

6.2No stop, moved stops and averaging down✓

Three versions of the same mistake: refusing to take a small loss.

  • No stop. "I'll watch it." Then you are asleep, or at work, or frozen.
  • Moving the stop away. Price comes near it, so you give it "a bit more room". Then a bit more.
  • Averaging down a losing trade. Buying more because it is cheaper now. The position grows exactly as the evidence against it grows.
What one unchecked loss costs

You win four trades at +1R each: +4R. On the fifth you move the stop twice and finally close at −4R.

Five trades, 80% win rate, and the result is zero. With fees it is a loss.

Note the difference from DCA in module 5. DCA is a plan made in advance, without leverage, into an asset you intend to hold for years. Averaging down is an unplanned reaction inside a trade that already has a stop it should have hit.

Remember

The first loss is the cheapest one. Take it and move to the next trade.

6.3Overtrading and what fees really cost✓

More trades feel like more work done. Usually they are just more fees and more mistakes.

Fees on an active month

Spot fee 0.1% per side, so 0.2% for a round trip. Position size $1,000.

  • 5 trades a day × 20 days = 100 round trips
  • 100 × $2 = $200 in fees

On a $2,000 account that is 10% of the account, paid before any trade wins or loses. A swing trader making 5 trades that month pays $10.

Signs you are overtrading:

  • You enter because you are bored or because the chart is open.
  • You cannot say which of your setups this trade is.
  • You trade more after a loss than after a win.
  • You switch to a lower timeframe to "find something".

The fix is mechanical: a fixed maximum of trades per day or week, and only the setups written in your plan.

6.4Following blindly, and a final checklist✓

No signal, group or person removes your responsibility for a trade. That includes the AI signals on this site: they are ideas with a tracked record, wins and losses both shown, and they are still only ideas. Check each one against your own plan and size it with your own risk rule.

Walk away when you see:

  • Guaranteed returns, or a win rate that is "99%".
  • Screenshots of profits, but no full record with the losses.
  • Pressure to hurry: "last spots", "entry closes in 10 minutes".
  • Requests to send money to be traded for you, or for a seed phrase.
  • A paid "VIP" that is always one tier above what you bought.

Also give any method a fair test. Changing strategy after three losses means you never see what any of them can do. Judge after 30–50 trades.

Before every trade
  • Does the higher timeframe agree with my direction?
  • Is the stop where the idea is wrong, and is it placed in the exchange?
  • Is my risk 1% (2% at most) of the account?
  • Is the reward at least 1.5 times the risk?
  • Is my total open risk under 4%, and am I inside my daily limit?
  • Is it written in my journal?

Six times yes, or no trade.
